## How a biometric gun safe reads your fingerprint (and why it misses)

**A biometric gun safe compares a fresh scan of your finger against templates stored during enrollment, and it opens only when enough ridge detail matches.** A wet, dirty, cut or cold finger changes that detail, which is why every fingerprint reader produces false rejects.

False rejects are the complaint that shows up for every model in this guide, from the premium Vaultek to the budget boxes. The fix is mostly in how you enroll. Each print takes several registration scans (Vaultek's 20 Series support page says five), and owners who store the same finger multiple times at slightly different angles report fewer misses.

We would set up any fingerprint safe this way:

1. Enroll your primary finger at least twice, once flat and once tilted the way you reach in the dark.
2. Enroll the same finger on your other hand in case one hand is injured or busy.
3. Keep the sensor clean; a smudged reader misses more often than a dirty finger.
4. Test entry with dry and slightly damp hands before you trust it at night.

## Backup entry and power: what happens when the battery dies

**When the battery dies, a biometric gun safe stays locked and the fingerprint reader goes dark, so your backup key or emergency power port becomes the only way in.** That is why we look at power before we look at features.

Power falls into three groups across our picks. Plug-in safes like the Vaultek DS2i run on wall power with small backup batteries. Battery safes like the LifePod 20 (9V) and the Barska (4 AA) run for months on alkaline cells. Rechargeable boxes like the BILLCONCH need charging, but give a USB port for a power bank.

The weak point is the backup key. A key locked inside the safe, or lost in a junk drawer, turns a dead battery into a locksmith call. Keep one key somewhere you can reach and children can't, and swap batteries on a fixed date rather than waiting for the low-battery beep.

> **From our notes:** When we lined up the six safes by backup entry, the pattern was hard to miss. The cheapest boxes all had a fingerprint reader, a keypad and a key, which looks complete on paper. What separated them was power: only the Vaultek DS2i plugs into the wall, only the LifePod quotes a year of battery life, and the Barska is the only one with a plug-in battery pack. The recall notice changed how we read the rest. A reader that fails to open for you is an annoyance; one that opens for someone else is the real danger.

## The biometric safe recall you should know about

**In 2024 the CPSC recalled over 120,000 biometric gun safes from four brands because the fingerprint lock could open for unauthorized users, including children.** One of those brands, awesafe, is still among the most recommended budget safes, so this matters for anyone shopping the low end.

The [CPSC recall notice](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2024/Biometric-Gun-Safes-Recalled-Due-to-Serious-Injury-Hazard-and-Risk-of-Death-Imported-by-Awesafe) covers about 60,000 awesafe safes sold at Walmart and on Amazon and Walmart.com from August 2019 through December 7, 2022. The same wave included Bulldog, Machir and BBRKIN models. If you buy any biometric safe secondhand, look up the brand and model on cpsc.gov first, and test that an unenrolled finger is rejected before you store a gun in it.

## How to choose a fingerprint gun safe

**Choose a fingerprint gun safe by what it stores, where it lives and how you get in when the reader fails.** Price and app features come after those three.

Run through these points before you buy, and see our [safe buying checklist](/safe-buying-checklist/) for the full list:

- **What you store:** measure the gun with its light or optic; the DS2i drawer takes up to 9 inches, the LifePod about 9.25 inches.
- **Backup entry:** a keypad you can use by feel, plus a key kept outside the safe.
- **Power:** plug-in for a nightstand with an outlet, long-life batteries for a car.
- **Steel and anchoring:** none of these are burglary safes; bolt or cable them down. A lower steel gauge number means thicker steel.
- **Fire:** none of our picks claim a fire rating, so documents belong in a separately rated fire safe.
- **Recall status:** check the brand on cpsc.gov.

## Setting up and living with a biometric safe

**Set up a biometric safe once with care, anchor it, and then check the battery and backup key on a schedule.** Most bad owner experiences trace back to rushed enrollment or a forgotten battery.

Bolt the safe to a nightstand, floor or shelf where the hardware allows, or loop the security cable around something solid; a 3 to 10 lb box is easy to carry away. Re-enroll if your skin changes with the season or work, and re-check that the drawer or lid opens smoothly after you move the safe.

Storage rules vary by state and city. We don't give legal advice, so check your state and local firearm storage laws, and if you live in California, look up the model on the [California DOJ roster of certified firearm safety devices](https://oag.ca.gov/firearms/fsdcertlist). If a safe that isn't yours is locked, contact the maker or a locksmith rather than trying to defeat it.
