## Our quick verdict on the best pistol safe

**The best pistol safe for most people is the Fort Knox PB1 Original Pistol Box, because a 10-gauge steel box with a Simplex mechanical lock keeps working when batteries and electronics fail.** The other three picks win on a specific job: fingerprint access, touch-free RFID entry or travel.

Here is how the four picks split up by job:

- **Best overall:** Fort Knox PB1, a mechanical pistol box with the thickest steel here.
- **Best biometric:** Vaultek DS2i Smart Station, a fingerprint drawer that doubles as a phone charger.
- **Best RFID access:** Hornady RAPiD Safe 2700KP, opened by a wristband, fob or sticker.
- **Best for travel:** Vaultek LifePod 20, a light, airtight case with a steel cable.

A pistol safe is a small, quick-access box sized for one handgun and maybe a spare magazine. It sits in a nightstand, a desk drawer or a car, and it trades the capacity and fire protection of a full gun safe for speed. Every pick below has a backup entry method, and in our view that matters more than any feature list.

## The best pistol safes, reviewed

**Each pistol safe below earns its place for a different reason, so read the "who it's for" line first and match it to where your handgun will live.** We list owner praise and complaints in plain words, because the failures owners report are the details spec sheets leave out.

### Fort Knox PB1 Original Pistol Box: Best overall

Built like a small vault, the [Fort Knox PB1 Original Pistol Box](amzn:Fort+Knox+PB1+Original+Pistol+Box) pairs a 10-gauge steel body with a 3/16-inch wraparound door, per Gunsafes.com and other retailer listings. A Simplex mechanical push-button lock opens it with no batteries, and a gas strut lifts the lid once the code is in. Listings put the exterior at about 12.5 by 10.4 by 4.25 inches, and the weight at 22 to 24 pounds.

Owners on Walmart and Gunsafes.com describe boxes that still work after six, seven and even fifteen years, and one Walmart reviewer says cheaper electronic boxes wore out while the PB1 did not. The complaints are about mass and flexibility: it is heavy and hard-cornered, and review sites note the Simplex combination cannot be changed at home without Fort Knox support or a locksmith. The PB1 is sold as California DOJ certified and carries a Fort Knox lifetime warranty. There is no fire rating.

We would bolt this box into a nightstand or closet floor and practice the code by feel until it is automatic.

### Vaultek DS2i Smart Station: Best biometric

Disguised as a bedside charging dock, the [Vaultek DS2i Smart Station](amzn:Vaultek+DS2i+Smart+Station) hides an auto-open drawer that slides out on ball-bearing rails. You get four ways in: a fingerprint reader, a backlit four-digit keypad, the Vaultek Bluetooth app and two backup keys. Fingerprint capacity is listed inconsistently, with Vaultek's own announcement saying 20 prints and some retailers saying 80.

Per Vaultek and retailer listings, the drawer fits one full-size handgun up to 9 inches long plus a spare magazine, the exterior measures about 12.1 by 8.25 by 3 inches, and the unit weighs about 9.6 pounds. It runs on its AC adapter, with two AAA batteries for short-term backup power, and charges a phone wirelessly on top.

Owners on Scheels and review blogs praise fast access once fingerprints are enrolled well. The recurring complaints are a reader that misses when the finger is angled differently, a charging pad that is fussy about phone placement, and app connection drops. Owners also say the unit slides when the drawer closes, so we would mount it to the nightstand.

### Hornady RAPiD Safe 2700KP: Best RFID access

Touch-free entry sets the [Hornady RAPiD Safe 2700KP](amzn:Hornady+RAPiD+Safe+2700KP) apart: wave a programmed RFID wristband, key fob or sticker over the lid and it pops open. Hornady says up to five tags can be programmed, with a four- to six-digit keypad and two barrel keys as backups. The housing is 14-gauge steel with two hardened internal locking lugs, per retailer listings and an American Rifleman review.

Hornady lists the exterior at 12.7 by 8.7 by 2.9 inches and the interior at 9 by 7.7 by 2.2 inches. Power comes from four AA batteries or the included AC adapter, and the included steel cable lets you tether it in a car. Retailers list it as meeting TSA requirements for checked luggage.

On OpticsPlanet, owners like the speed of the wristband and the AC adapter. The complaints: some tags read slowly, one owner found the discs unreliable, and on battery power you press a button to wake the reader first. One reviewer also points out that a child who watches you wave the wristband learns how to open it, so we suggest keeping tags on you, not on the nightstand.

### Vaultek LifePod 20: Best for travel

Sold as the LifePod 2.0, the [Vaultek LifePod 20](amzn:Vaultek+LifePod+20+Full-Size+Lockable+Weatherproof+Case) is a lockable, airtight case rather than a steel safe. Listings give an exterior of 12.25 by 8.5 by 2.875 inches, an interior that fits a handgun up to 9.25 inches long, and a weight of about 3.3 pounds. A backlit keypad opens it, two backup keys come with it, and the keyway can be disabled to resist picking.

A 9V battery is rated for up to a year, with a micro-USB port for emergency power. Vaultek markets it as weather resistant and TSA compliant, and the Amazon listing includes a steel security cable. Owners on a Northwest Firearms forum thread call it practical and reliable for travel, and one says the first 9V lasted just over a year. The same owner says it is a theft deterrent and not true quick-access storage.

We would use it as the car and hotel box, looped to a seat frame, and keep a real pistol safe at home.

## How to choose a pistol safe

**Choose a pistol safe by where it will live and how you will open it under stress, then check steel, fit and anchoring.** Our [safe buying checklist](/safe-buying-checklist/) walks through the same points for every kind of safe.

### Lock type and backup entry

A mechanical lock like the Simplex has no battery to die and opens by feel. Biometric locks are fast when they read well, but owners of several models report misses when a finger is placed at a different angle. RFID entry is touch-free but depends on keeping the tag on you. Whatever the main lock, the backup entry decides what happens when it fails: a keypad, a key, or both. We always tell readers the same thing: never store the backup key inside the safe, and never somewhere a child will find it.

### Steel gauge and pry resistance

Steel gauge numbers go down as the steel gets thicker, so 10-gauge is much heavier plate than 16- or 18-gauge. Thin electronic boxes keep curious kids out but can be pried by an adult with tools. A wraparound door, internal lugs and no exposed hinges all help. The Fort Knox PB1 (10-gauge) and the Hornady 2700KP (14-gauge) are the sturdiest builds here.

### Interior size and handgun fit

Measure your handgun with any light or optic attached before you buy. The DS2i drawer fits a pistol up to 9 inches long and the LifePod 20 fits one up to 9.25 inches, per their listings. Interior height matters too: a box around 2 inches tall will not take a tall magazine extension.

### Fire protection

Fire ratings come from labs such as UL (the UL 72 standard) or Intertek, and they state a duration and temperature. None of these pistol safes has one, and that is normal for the category. A fire rating is not burglary protection, and burglary protection is not a fire rating, so documents and backup drives belong in a separately rated fire safe.

## Setting up and anchoring a pistol safe

**A pistol safe only stops theft once it is anchored, because an unbolted box can be carried away and opened later.** Setup takes an evening, and the steps are the same for most models.

1. Read the manual and set the code or enroll fingerprints. For fingerprint readers, enroll the same finger several times at different angles, as DS2i owners recommend.
2. Program any RFID tags or app access, then test every entry method, including the backup key.
3. Choose the spot: inside a nightstand, on a closet shelf or under a desk, reachable from where you sleep.
4. Bolt the safe to a stud, the floor or heavy furniture using its mounting holes. For portable boxes, loop the steel cable around a fixed point.
5. Practice opening it in the dark until it is automatic.

Mounting hardware varies by model, so check what is in the box before you start. In a car, a cable tethered to the seat frame is the usual setup for a portable case.

> **From our notes:** When we lined up the spec sheets for these four pistol safes, the backup entry turned out to be the line that separated them. The Fort Knox PB1 needs none because its Simplex lock has no battery. The Vaultek DS2i leans on two AAA cells for short-term backup when its AC power is cut, and the Hornady 2700KP asks for a button press before the RFID reader wakes on batteries. Reading owner reviews, the failures people describe were rarely the steel; they were readers, chargers and tags. That is why we rank the mechanical box first.

## Pistol safe care and legal storage

**A pistol safe needs a little care to stay reliable: fresh batteries, a clean reader and a check of the bolts every few months.** Replace batteries on a schedule instead of waiting for the low-battery warning, wipe fingerprint readers clean, and re-test the backup key twice a year.

Firearm storage rules differ by state and city, and some require an approved locking device or set rules for homes with children. We are not giving legal advice here: check your state and local firearm storage laws, and if you live in California, check the [California DOJ roster of firearm safety devices](https://oag.ca.gov/firearms/fsdcertlist) for the exact model. Flying with a handgun has its own rules, which the [TSA explains on its firearms page](https://www.tsa.gov/travel/transporting-firearms-and-ammunition). For family storage habits, [Project ChildSafe](https://projectchildsafe.org/) has free guidance.
