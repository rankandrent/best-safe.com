To move a gun safe, empty it, unbolt it, wrap it, and roll it upright on an appliance dolly rated above its weight, strapped tight, with at least three people. If the route has stairs or the safe weighs [more than 500 lb](https://www.kirbysmovingco.com/blog/gun-safe-moving-checklist) (Kirby's Moving's cutoff), the best way to move a gun safe is to hire a safe mover with a stair climber.

In owner threads, household movers often refuse the safe.

## Short answer – how to move a gun safe

**A gun safe move is safe when the gear is rated for the weight, the route is measured, and stairs go to a pro.** General guidance, not a rule from any one maker:

- **Under 500 lb, flat route, no stairs:** DIY is workable with a heavy-duty appliance dolly, ratchet straps and at least three helpers.
- **Any stairs, or over 500 lb:** hire professional movers who specialize in safes. U-Haul says stairs should "always be done by professionals," and Kirby's Moving gives 500 lb as its hire-a-pro line.
- **Heavier than your dolly's rating:** it's a crew job. Owners put heavy-duty dollies near 800 lb, and Liberty Safe says its Fatboy Jr. Extreme needs one rated for at least 800 lb.

## Before you touch it – weight, route and where the guns go

**Moving a gun safe starts with three numbers: its empty weight, its depth with the handle on, and the narrowest doorway on the route.** Get the weight from the maker's spec page, then measure doorways, halls and every turn. Liberty Safe notes that a home-size gun safe with composite or concrete insulation [may weigh up to 3,100 pounds](https://libertysafe.com/blogs/the-vault/tips-for-moving-a-gun-safe-on-your-own).

### Empty the safe and keep the firearms legal

Empty the safe completely; contents add weight and shift. Meanwhile, the guns still need secure storage: a locked case or a smaller safe such as one from our [pistol safe guide](/blog/best-pistol-safe/), unloaded, with ammunition separate. Storage and transport rules vary by state and city, so check your state's official firearm storage page; we can't give legal advice.

## Gear that matters – dolly rating, sliders, straps and blankets

**The single biggest mistake is rating the gear to "a dolly" instead of to the safe's weight.** One owner on The High Road put the [dolly limit](https://www.thehighroad.org/index.php?threads/winchester-safe.917886/) plainly: "Ordinary appliance dolly will max out at 600#; heavy duty dolly right at 800#". Liberty Safe's own moving guide calls for a dolly that can hold [at least 800 lb](https://libertysafe.com/blogs/the-vault/tips-for-moving-a-gun-safe-on-your-own) for its Fatboy Jr. Extreme model, and bigger safes can outweigh both.

The checklist we'd assemble:

1. **Heavy-duty appliance dolly**, rated above the empty weight, with a strap.
2. **Ratchet straps** for the dolly and the truck.
3. **Furniture sliders**, plastic for carpet and felt for hard floors.
4. **Moving blankets and stretch wrap** for the finish and frames.
5. **A ramp** rated for the safe plus the dolly, if the truck has no lift gate.

## Step-by-step – moving a gun safe from the closet to the truck

**Moving a gun safe is a slow sequence – slide, tilt, strap, roll – with hands on it the whole time.** Tractor Supply's video below walks through the same tools and teamwork.

1. Unbolt it. Kirby's Moving lists forgetting that the safe is [bolted down](https://www.kirbysmovingco.com/blog/gun-safe-moving-checklist) as mistake #8; look inside for bolts into the slab or framing.
2. Close and lock the door so it cannot swing open mid-move.
3. Wrap the safe in moving blankets and stretch wrap.
4. Tip one side, slip furniture sliders under, and slide it out of the closet.
5. Tilt it back a few inches, push the dolly nose under, and cinch the ratchet straps.
6. Roll with one person on the handle, one per side, and a guide ahead.

Keep it upright. Whether yours can lie on its back or side is a question for the maker's manual, not a guess.

### Never use the safe handle

Many handles are break-away or clutch designs, so never lift, pull or lever the safe by one.

### Loading and securing in the vehicle

Use a rated ramp or lift gate with people on both sides, then strap the safe upright to the vehicle. Liberty Safe warns not to rely on the safe's weight to keep it from tipping.

## Problems owners run into, and the fix

**Most gun safe move failures happen at a stair, turn, ramp or threshold, not on flat floor.**

### Moving a gun safe up stairs and into basements

Stairs come up in nearly every thread. On a [High Road stairs thread](https://www.thehighroad.org/index.php?threads/transporting-a-safe-up-a-flight-of-stairs.797917/), one owner wrote: "Hire a professional. Don't rent a stair climbing dolly." The fix is an insured safe mover with a powered stair-climbing dolly; for a new purchase, a modular or smaller safe avoids it.

### Turns, doorways and dolly limits

The same thread names the failure nobody measures for: "the turn at the top won't allow any one-piece safe, even a medium size one, to be taken up or down." Measure the landing diagonally with the safe's depth plus handle. Removing the door, where the maker allows it, cuts weight; otherwise pick a safe that fits the route.

### Floor load and stair strength

For an upstairs spot, one High Road poster noted, "Most residential floors are only rated for about 40 lb per sq ft." Another poster in that thread called it a design load spread across the whole room, not a limit per spot, and a loaded safe concentrates hundreds of pounds on a few square feet, and an [r/Safes thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/Safes/comments/1ftyphw/moving_a_600lb_safe_upstairs_to_its_final/) adds "be sure your stairs can handle the weight". Heavy [fire-rated safes](/blog/best-fireproof-safe/) raise the stakes. Ask a structural engineer or your building department; this is general information, not engineering or legal advice.

### Injuries, quotes and when to pay a mover

Back injuries, broken feet and dropped safes recur. On [AccurateShooter](https://forum.accurateshooter.com/threads/safe-moving-cost.4155950/page-2) one owner wrote: "One good back injury with a do-it-yourself move and you sure will regret not paying a professional at any cost." Another, on the S&W forum, dropped a safe on the way down a rental truck ramp using a refrigerator cart. Paid prices vary widely with weight, stairs and distance, so get several quotes and give each mover both addresses and the empty weight.

> **From our notes:** When we sorted the owner threads for this guide by what went wrong, the pattern surprised us. Few of the reports involved trouble on a flat floor. The injuries, the dropped safe and the gouged frames all came at a transition: a truck ramp, the first stair, a landing turn, a threshold strip. So we stopped treating safe moving as one long push and wrote the steps as a handful of risky moments, each needing a spotter.

### Frames, thresholds and rental deposits

A heavy safe can tear up "door frames and thresholds," as one [S&W forum](https://smith-wessonforum.com/threads/safe-moving.733512/) owner warned. Renters feel it most; a [FirearmsTalk](https://www.firearmstalk.com/threads/gun-safe-on-the-second-floor-apartment.121162/) poster said a pro "Might save your thresholds, steps, security deposit, etc." Photograph frames and floors first, lay hardboard over finished floors, wrap jambs in blankets, and check your lease or ask the landlord in writing before bolting into a rental floor.

## After the move – level, bolt down and recheck the lock

**A gun safe move isn't done until the safe is level, anchored and opening normally.** Liberty Safe's guide says to bolt it down again, and its video below shows how its dealers deliver and install a safe.

### Re-anchor and check the door and lock

Shim it level so the door doesn't swing, then bolt into the slab or framing. Then open and close the door, throw the bolts, and confirm the lock and backup entry work. In a basement, add humidity control from our [gun safe dehumidifier guide](/blog/best-dehumidifier-for-gun-safe/).
