## Our quick verdict on the best fireproof document safe

**A fireproof document safe for most households should carry an independent UL fire rating, a separate water rating and an interior that fits letter-size paper without folding.** Our three picks all come from SentrySafe, because its models are the ones the top guides and owner threads keep returning to, and its ratings are spelled out per model.

- **Best overall:** SentrySafe SFW123DSB, UL Classified 1 hour at 1700°F, ETL water rating, steel body.
- **Best waterproof file box:** SentrySafe HD4100, UL Classified 1/2 hour at 1550°F, ETL Verified for 72 hours under water.
- **Best budget box:** SentrySafe 1170, UL Classified 1/2 hour at 1550°F, ETL media rating, no water seal.

A fire-rated document box is not a burglary safe. None of these has a burglary rating, so think of them as protection against heat and water first.

## What fire rating a fireproof document safe needs

**For paper, a fireproof document safe should carry a UL fire rating of at least 30 minutes, and an hour if it sits where a fire could burn long, such as an upstairs closet or near a garage.** The duration and the test temperature together tell you how much fire it was built to outlast.

### Paper vs digital media ratings

The UL 72 standard groups fire-rated record protection into classes. Class 350 keeps the interior at or below 350°F, the limit for paper records. Class 125 keeps it at or below 125°F and 80% humidity, which flash drives and discs need. That gap explains why a box that saves your passport can still cook a USB stick. Of our picks, only the 1170 lists a separate media rating (ETL, 30 minutes).

### UL, ETL or the maker's word

UL Classified means UL ran the fire test. ETL marks come from Intertek, an independent lab, and on these models they cover water, drop and media claims. A rating with no lab named is the maker's own claim. The Washington State Archives makes the same point bluntly: no safe is truly fire-proof, only rated to resist heat for a time. That is why we always cite the lab and the exact number.

## Will your papers fit? Letter, legal and file folders

**A fireproof document safe fits letter-size papers if one interior dimension is at least 11 inches; legal-size needs 14 inches.** Measure before you buy, because marketing photos rarely show folder clearance.

Here is how the three compare for common documents:

1. Letter-size sheets (8.5 x 11 in) stand upright in all three.
2. Legal-size sheets (8.5 x 14 in) need folding in all three.
3. Hanging folders suit the HD4100 and 1170, both sold for them.
4. Passports, birth certificates and USB drives fit anywhere, but the drives are better off with a media rating.

If legal papers are your main load, shop for a chest built for legal hanging files and check its interior width, not just its cubic feet.

## Small chest or file box or full safe

**A small fireproof box or chest suits a few passports and certificates, a file box suits folders, and a steel safe suits papers plus valuables you want anchored.** Chests are light and portable, which helps in an evacuation, but most are plastic shells with privacy locks. File boxes like the HD4100 and 1170 add hanging-file space. A steel safe like the SFW123DSB adds bolts and bolt-down hardware but weighs far more.

Ask yourself one question: are you protecting against fire and water, or also against someone carrying the box off? If theft matters, the steel safe, anchored to the floor, is the only pick here that addresses it, and even it has no burglary rating.

## Setting up and caring for a document safe

**Set up a fireproof document safe by placing it low, keeping papers in sealed bags, and adding a desiccant to control condensation.** Sealed fire safes trap moisture, which owners of the SFW123DSB and other models report as damp papers or rust.

A few habits help:

- Put papers in zip-top bags so a little moisture does no harm.
- Swap or recharge the silica gel pack every few months.
- Store spare keys and the combination outside the safe, with someone you trust.
- Keep scans or copies of key papers in a second place, such as cloud storage or a safe deposit box.
- Re-check the gasket on waterproof boxes each year for cracks or gaps.

We also tell readers to open the box now and then. That airs it out and confirms the lock still works before the day you need it.
