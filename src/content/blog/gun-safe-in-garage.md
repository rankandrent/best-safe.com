Picture a new gun safe on the garage slab, the garage door rolling up beside it every morning. **A gun safe in the garage works if you keep the inside dry, lift it off the cold concrete, bolt it down and keep it out of view from the street.** Before the safe arrives, we'd plan for condensation, theft and fire.

## Can you keep a gun safe in the garage

**Yes; a gun safe in the garage is common, and Northwest Safe says most of its own staff keep one there.** The guides we read name the same risks: moisture, temperature swings and theft. The EPA says damp materials dried within [24-48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home) usually won't grow mold; a sealed safe rarely dries that fast.

## Why a gun safe in the garage sweats, and the fix

**A gun safe in the garage sweats when its steel is colder than the air, so the fix is a little heat inside, not more silica.** "Sounds like the outside wall is chilling the safe and causing condensation, like dew on a toilet bowl," one [AR15.com reply](https://ar15.com/forums/general/humidity-problems-in-the-gunsafe--help-needed/5-2079886/) said. A Hunt Talk owner in cold Idaho noted that cold and dry is fine; the humidity that comes with temperature changes is the problem.

Here is how we'd keep humidity in the safe under control:

1. Keep the safe off exterior walls.
2. Run a dehumidifier rod at the bottom of the safe. It needs an outlet and should feel warm; one Shooters' Forum owner's rod never got hot and his reading sat at 65 percent.
3. Add a rechargeable desiccant, Northwest Safe's second layer, since it works through a power cut.
4. Hold relative humidity [below 60 percent](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home), ideally 30 to 50, per the EPA; a hygrometer inside tells you.

In the video below, a Liberty Safe rep says a 12-inch rod covers 100 cubic feet. For picks by size, see our [best dehumidifier for gun safe](/blog/best-dehumidifier-for-gun-safe/) guide.

## What goes under the safe on a concrete floor

**Don't set the safe flat on bare concrete; give it an airflow gap or a barrier.**

> **From our notes:** When we lined up the maker and dealer advice for this guide, the floor question didn't agree. Northwest Safe levels garage safes on wood and composite shims to leave an airflow pocket, and says a rubber mat or plywood traps moisture. Liberty Safe suggests an epoxied floor or a rubber mat before bolting. Owners split too: one Hunt Talk member spaced his safe on thin PVC strips, another swears by a vapor barrier. We lean toward airflow plus a barrier, and checking under the safe after the first wet season.

## Bolting a gun safe to the garage slab

**Bolt the safe to the slab through its floor holes, after you know what slab you have.** Northwest Safe typically uses a [3/8-inch bit and a 3 x 3/8-inch bolt](https://nwsafe.com/blogs/tips/put-a-gun-safe-in-the-garage); the holes patch if you move.

A High Road reply says "the number one issue you need to check first, is how thick a slab the garage has." On a post-tension slab, AR15.com owners agree: "OP you're not going to get anyone who recommends drilling into a PT slab." Scan for cables or anchor to studs. See our guide on [how to bolt down a safe](/blog/how-to-bolt-down-a-safe/) for both cases.

## Garage theft and keeping the safe out of sight

**A gun safe in the garage is easier to spot and to work on than one inside, so hide it and anchor it hard.** "A gun safe in the garage is a burgular's dream," one [Shooters' Forum member](https://forum.accurateshooter.com/threads/a-gun-safe-in-the-garage.3814148/) warned: a thief can pull in, close the door and work unseen. A Hunt Talk owner added: "Make sure it can't be seen from the street when the door is open."

From those threads, our short list:

- Tuck it behind a short wing wall, out of the open door's view (a High Road tip).
- Bolt it to the floor and the wall.
- Enclose it in a closet so there's no room to work on it.
- Add an alarm to the garage or the safe.

## Garage fires and the fire rating

**Garage fires burn bigger, so a garage safe's fire rating matters, and so does who certified it.** USFA counts about [6,600 residential garage fires](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/statistics/v14i12.pdf) a year, which tend to spread farther, fed by paint, chemicals and ammunition. A rating certified by UL or Intertek (ETL) comes from a third-party test; one based on the maker's own test is a claim. Liberty notes fireboard absorbs moisture, so humidity control matters more. See our [best fire rated gun safe](/blog/best-fire-rated-gun-safe/) guide for each certifier.

## Gun safe in the basement instead

**A basement is steadier than a garage only if it stays dry.** Liberty warns basements can flood and suggests a sump pump with a regularly tested backup battery. If you raise the safe on a base, Liberty notes a thief can jack it off; we'd anchor a steel base to the floor and the safe to the base.

For the stairs: "Hire a professional. Don't rent a stair climbing dolly," one High Road owner advised. See our guide on [how much a gun safe weighs](/blog/how-much-does-a-gun-safe-weigh/) does the loaded-weight floor math.

## Problems owners run into, and the fix

**Most garage-safe trouble we found comes from three repeat mistakes.**

- **Silica that saturates fast.** One Hunt Talk owner recharged his every other day, then switched to a small heater.
- **Gun socks and felt that hold damp.** "Get rid of the gun socks, they attract moisture and don't allow for air to move evaporating moisture," a High Road reply said.
- **A room dehumidifier that freezes up.** One High Road owner's room unit froze up in cold weather despite a defrost feature.
