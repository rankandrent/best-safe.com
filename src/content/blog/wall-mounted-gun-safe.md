It's 2 a.m., the kids are asleep down the hall, and the home-defense pistol needs to be off the nightstand but still a few steps from the bed. That's the job of a wall mounted gun safe: a steel box screwed into the studs, hidden behind a picture, opened by code or fingerprint. **For most homes we'd pick the SnapSafe In-Wall Safe 75413, a 22 in tall in-wall safe with an 8-gauge steel door, a keypad and a barrel backup key.** Its 44 in Tall sibling takes a carbine, the Barska opens to a fingerprint, and the Protex adds a power override for dead batteries. None of the four is fire rated.

## Our wall mounted gun safe picks

**A wall mounted gun safe should fit your stud bay, open by feel in the dark and screw into wood, not just drywall.** We picked the four models that independent roundups and best-seller lists recommend and that sell on Amazon US, then checked each against the maker's specs and manuals and what owners report.

### SnapSafe In-Wall Safe Tall 75414: Best for a carbine or shotgun

Choose this one when the gun is long. SnapSafe lists it at 44 x 16.25 x 4 in and 66 lb, with a [16-gauge body and an 8-gauge steel door](https://www.snapsafe.com/specialty-safes/in-wall-safes/) (a lower steel gauge number means thicker steel). Five shelves come out so an AR-15 carbine can stand on its stock, and eight lag screws go through the sides into the studs. A Reddit owner keeps a Glock 19 and an M&P15 rifle in one and recommends it.

The OpticsPlanet owner reviews are the most useful we found. Owners say the door is heavier than they expected and the cut dimensions are precise. Three of them also had to add washers at the door pivot so the door closed or opened smoothly, and one received chipped paint. Another owner's studs weren't 16 in on center, so the owner moved one an inch before the [SnapSafe In-Wall Safe Tall 75414](amzn:SnapSafe+In+Wall+Tall+Safe+75414) slid in.

### SnapSafe In-Wall Safe 75413: Best for handguns and papers

The 22 in version sits recessed between the studs with the same door, body and lock in half the height, which is why it's our top pick for most readers. It weighs 35 lb, needs a 20 in tall cut, and takes a handgun, a spare magazine and papers on two shelves, plus magnetic key hooks and a document holder. The door can be set to swing left or right.

Pro Tool Reviews installed one with an oscillating multi-tool and an impact driver and called it easy for anyone comfortable hanging a TV. It found a small gap between the safe and the studs, so the reviewer pushed the body against one stud before driving the lags. The [SnapSafe In-Wall Safe 75413](amzn:SnapSafe+In+Wall+Gun+Safe+75413) ships with a picture hook rated for 8 lb to hang art over the door.

### Barska Biometric Wall Safe AX12038: Best fingerprint entry

This is the pick for a fingerprint scanner set into the wall. Barska's optical reader stores 120 prints, two motorized deadbolts lock a 4 mm steel door on a 2 mm body, and two backup keys handle a dead battery. It's 20.75 x 15.43 x 3.73 in outside with 0.52 cu ft inside, and Barska lists the right-opening AX12038 as California DOJ approved (the left-opening twin is the AX13034).

An OpticsPlanet owner says it installs and programs easily and reads fingerprints well. The same owner found that the battery box and lock mechanism eat into the already shallow interior, and the door has little to grip. One Home Depot reviewer's safe arrived out of square with gaps at the welds, and Barska's warranty is one year. Read the [Barska Biometric Wall Safe AX12038](amzn:Barska+AX12038+Biometric+Wall+Safe) as a quick-access box, not a vault.

### Protex PWS-1814E Wall Safe: Best backup power option

The Protex suits buyers whose main fear is a dead battery. Protex lists two 3/4 in motorized live bolts, a spring-loaded door that opens itself on the right code, two override keys and a power override. It's 18.25 x 14.125 x 3.875 in outside with [about 0.44 cubic feet](https://www.protexsafe.com/wall-safes/pws-1814e-black.html) inside, and a 16 x 20 in flange hides the drywall cut.

A Garage Journal owner says his has worked fine for six years: "It has an external battery back plug in and a key for backup entry options should the internal batteries die." His complaint is the depth, which fits about four medium-frame pistols. Protex calls the steel heavy duty but publishes no gauge, so we score the [Protex PWS-1814E Wall Safe](amzn:Protex+PWS-1814E+Electronic+Keypad+Wall+Safe) below the SnapSafe on security.

It was depth: 4 in outside on both SnapSafes, 3.5 in inside on the Barska and 3 in on the Protex. A stud wall sets that limit for every brand. So before choosing a lock, we'd lay the pistol on the table with its light attached, or the carbine with its optic, and measure its thickest point against those numbers.

## Will your gun fit a wall mounted gun safe?

**Measure the gun's thickest point and your stud bay before you buy, because an in-wall safe can't be deeper than the wall.** SnapSafe's install steps say to [confirm 14.5 in between the studs](https://static.hornady.media/presscenter/docs/1410998004-Snap-Safe-In-Wall-Safes-Instructions1679325642.pdf), then cut a 20 or 42 in tall opening. The Garage Journal Protex owner sums up the trade-off: "I can fit about 4 medium framed pistols in there but it's not very deep."

Check these four things first:

1. **Stud spacing.** All four picks are sized for 16 in on-center studs. With wider spacing, add framing so both sides screw into wood.
2. **Depth.** Lay the handgun flat with its light, or the carbine with its optic, and measure against 4 in.
3. **What's in the bay.** Use a stud finder and check for wiring, pipes and ducts before the drywall saw comes out.
4. **Door swing.** The SnapSafe door reverses; Barska sells right- and left-opening versions.

The Pro Tool Reviews video below shows the whole SnapSafe install, from marking the studs to driving the lags.

## Can a burglar pry it out of the wall?

**A wall safe resists a quick pry, but its real defense is staying hidden, because thin bodies and lag screws only go so far.** On Garage Journal, one member wrote: "My Dad's house was burglarized and they just pulled the safe right out of the wall and took it with them." Another called a budget model's door "Thin, weak door on that one. Easily pried open."

That's why door steel and anchoring matter. The SnapSafe's 8-gauge door is much thicker than its 16-gauge sides, and each lag screw (five or eight) runs into a wall stud. Barska and Protex use motorized bolts on thinner bodies. Concealment does the rest: we'd hang art over the safe, keep it out of the master closet, and tell as few people as possible where it is. For a gun collection or valuables you can't lose, a heavy floor safe bolted down per our [guide to bolting down a safe](/blog/how-to-bolt-down-a-safe/) protects far more.

## Fast access in the dark, and keeping kids out

**Pick the entry you can work half asleep, then keep a backup that doesn't share the same battery.** An r/CAguns poster asked for "recommendations for wall-mounted safes that allow quicker, one-handed access" ([thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/CAguns/comments/1nsk1ad/wall_mounted_gun_safe/)), and on Sniper's Hide a parent wanted the home-defense gun reachable for his wife but out of his young children's hands. Here's how each pick handles that:

- **SnapSafe:** a 4-6 digit keypad on four AA batteries. Holding the 1 key for three seconds turns the tones off, and a tone plus LED warn of low batteries.
- **Barska:** a fingerprint opens it with one hand; we'd enroll at least two fingers in case one is wet or cut.
- **Protex:** the door swings open on the code, and the power override and keys cover a dead battery.

Some owners skip electronics entirely. A Sniper's Hide member who owned a V-Line wall safe said "It had the simplex lock which was super quick and always worked." Whichever you choose, keep the override keys outside the safe and away from children. Change the default code (SnapSafe ships with 1-2-3-4) before the gun goes in.

## Where a wall mounted gun safe should go

**Use an interior wall with nothing in the bay, and skip exterior walls where a cold surface invites condensation.** A Sniper's Hide installer put it simply: "I suggest doing the installation on an interior wall to help mitigate condensation issues." A shallow steel box can still trap damp air, so a rechargeable [gun safe dehumidifier](/blog/best-dehumidifier-for-gun-safe/) or desiccant helps.

Renters have a harder time. An in-wall safe means cutting drywall, which most leases forbid, so get the landlord's OK in writing or choose a surface-anchored safe instead. Our [gun safe for an apartment](/blog/best-gun-safe-for-apartment/) picks cover lease-friendly anchoring.

## What a wall safe won't do

**None of these four carries a fire rating, so treat each as theft and child-access storage, not document protection.** Safe and Vault Store notes that [fire-rated wall safes run 12 to 15 in deep](https://www.safeandvaultstore.com/blogs/expert-advice-on-safes-and-vault-doors/10-best-selling-wall-safes-on-the-market) with a UL-listed 1-hour rating, far deeper than a 2x4 wall. If papers need an independent UL 72 or Intertek rating, our [fire rated gun safe picks](/blog/best-fire-rated-gun-safe/) name who certified each rating.

Storage laws differ by state and city, so check your state and local firearm storage rules. California publishes a [roster of approved firearm safety devices](https://oag.ca.gov/firearms/fsd-chart-retail). This is general information, not legal advice.
